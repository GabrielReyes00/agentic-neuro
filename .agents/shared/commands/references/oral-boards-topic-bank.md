# Oral Boards Topic Bank

Use to seed cases for `/oral-boards`. Topic selection should be adapted to PGY level, memory gaps, current rotation, and user request.

## PGY Scaling

| Level | Expected Performance | Case Style |
|---|---|---|
| PGY-1 | Recognize emergencies, localize, get critical history/exam, request correct imaging/labs, start safe initial management, escalate clearly. | Ward, ED consult, ICU, postop check, grand-rounds pimping. Operative steps are anatomy/indication-level. |
| PGY-2/3 | Build differential, interpret imaging, know indications/contraindications, outline common operations and complications. | Consult-to-OR reasoning with common complications and classification systems. |
| PGY-4/5 | Defend operative versus nonoperative choices, compare approaches, manage perioperative complications, discuss evidence and outcomes. | Senior resident mock oral with controversy and complication rescue. |
| PGY-6/7 | Independent junior-staff judgment, case prioritization, resource constraints, complex comorbidity, own-case defense. | ABNS-style rotations with five-case pacing and less coaching. |
| Post-training | Full ABNS simulation: general, focus area, and case-log defense. | Strict board-mode, delayed feedback, patient-safety gate. |

## ABNS High-Yield Domains

General and emergency:
- Trauma craniotomy, epidural/subdural hematoma, depressed skull fracture.
- Intracerebral hemorrhage from hypertensive, vascular, tumor, anticoagulation, amyloid, or other causes.
- Acute stroke care, malignant MCA infarction, hemicraniectomy, cerebellar stroke/suboccipital decompression.
- Vascular dissection and atherosclerotic vascular disease.
- CNS infection: abscess, empyema, ventriculitis, osteomyelitis, wound infection.
- Intratumoral hemorrhage and pituitary apoplexy.
- Cauda equina syndrome.
- Spinal cord injury and spinal fracture management.
- Hydrocephalus, shunt malfunction, ETV decisions.
- Neurology mimics of surgical disease: MS, temporal arteritis, seizure, stroke mimic.
- Baclofen pump failure, withdrawal, overdose.
- Peripheral nerve disorders and traumatic nerve injury.

Spine:
- Degenerative cervical myelopathy, radiculopathy, lumbar stenosis, disc herniation.
- Spinal tumors: intradural extramedullary, intramedullary, metastatic epidural compression.
- Spinal vascular malformations.
- Deformity basics, instrumentation failure, infection, adjacent segment disease.
- Trauma: C-spine fracture patterns, thoracolumbar burst fracture, central cord syndrome.

Tumor:
- Glioma management, brain metastasis, meningioma, vestibular schwannoma.
- Brain mapping, awake craniotomy indications, language/motor risks.
- Pineal region, intraventricular, skull base, pituitary/sellar, endoscopic surgery.
- CNS lymphoma, pediatric tumors.

Vascular:
- Aneurysmal SAH, clipping/endovascular choices, vasospasm, hydrocephalus.
- AVM, dural AVF, cavernous malformation.
- Ischemic stroke, carotid endarterectomy, bypass, moyamoya.
- Dissection, atherosclerotic disease, intracranial stenosis.

Functional, epilepsy, pain:
- DBS for Parkinson disease or essential tremor.
- Epilepsy workup, temporal lobectomy, stereo-EEG, neuromodulation.
- Trigeminal neuralgia, pain procedures, baclofen pump complications.
- Behavioral indications only at senior level.

Trauma and critical care:
- ICP management, CPP/MAP reasoning, hyperosmolar therapy, sedation, EVD, decompression.
- Secondary injury prevention, seizure prophylaxis, DVT prophylaxis timing, anticoagulation reversal.
- Systemic injuries and perioperative risk.

Pediatric:
- Hydrocephalus, shunt failure, ETV/CPC basics.
- Myelomeningocele, Chiari, craniosynostosis, tethered cord.
- Pediatric tumors, trauma, infection, congenital anomalies.

## Common Complication Cases

Complications are a major oral-board component. Seed these as twists or standalone cases:

- Postoperative wound infection or osteomyelitis.
- CSF leak or pseudomeningocele.
- C5 palsy.
- Postoperative cranial or spinal hematoma.
- Hyponatremia after cranial surgery or SAH.
- Vasospasm after aneurysmal SAH.
- Intraoperative aneurysm rupture.
- Major arterial injury during cranial or spine surgery.
- Uncontrolled ICP or intraoperative brain swelling.
- Esophageal injury after anterior cervical surgery.
- New postoperative neurologic deficit.
- Shunt infection or distal catheter complication.

## High-Consequence Zebras and Discriminators

Use sparingly. The learner should first handle common dangerous diagnoses safely.

- Spinal dural AVF masquerading as myelopathy.
- Pituitary apoplexy presenting as SAH/meningitis/ophthalmoplegia.
- CNS lymphoma worsened diagnostically by premature steroids.
- Temporal arteritis or MS mimicking surgical disease.
- Baclofen withdrawal versus sepsis/NMS-like syndrome.
- Moyamoya in ischemic or hemorrhagic presentation.
- Dural AVF causing pulsatile tinnitus, myelopathy, or hemorrhage.
- Cerebral venous sinus thrombosis with hemorrhage.
- Odontoid pannus or rheumatoid cervical instability.
- Normal pressure hydrocephalus versus Parkinsonism/dementia.
- Brown-Sequard, central cord, anterior cord, conus, cauda equina localizations.
- Pediatric nonaccidental trauma.
- Colloid cyst with intermittent hydrocephalus.
- Chiari/syrinx/tethered cord overlap.

## Oral-Board One-Liners To Reward

- "My first priority is to identify and treat immediately reversible threats while keeping the differential broad."
- "I would not give steroids before tissue diagnosis if primary CNS lymphoma is a serious consideration unless there is life-threatening mass effect."
- "The decision is not just whether surgery can be done, but whether the expected natural history and patient goals justify the risk."
- "If the patient deteriorates, I reassess ABCs, neurologic exam, imaging, and reversible causes before anchoring on the original diagnosis."
- "I would call my chief/attending early because this is a time-sensitive neurosurgical emergency, while I begin the safe initial steps."
