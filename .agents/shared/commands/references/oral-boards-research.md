# Oral Boards Research Notes

Use this as background for `/oral-boards`; do not load it unless building or auditing a board-style case.

## ABNS Format and Scoring Priorities

- The ABNS oral exam is the final certification step after residency/fellowship; it is usually taken 3-4 years after training and is administered twice yearly. Source: https://abns.org/oral-examination/
- Current ABNS oral format: three 45-minute rotations: general neurosurgery, a chosen focus area, and selected cases from the candidate's submitted case log. Each rotation has five scenarios and two examiners. Source: https://abns.org/oral-examination/
- The ABNS states scenarios may occur in the ED, office, or ICU and include history/exam, requested tests/images, differential, initial management, procedural plan when applicable, perioperative or intraoperative complications, postoperative management, and complications. Source: https://abns.org/oral-examination/
- The stated examiner emphasis is breadth of knowledge, patient safety, clinical judgment, management plan, and rationale. Reasonable alternative solutions may be acceptable if safe and justified. Source: https://abns.org/oral-examination/
- ABNS scoring uses psychometric analysis adjusted for examiner/question severity. An unsafe answer can result in failure. Source: https://abns.org/oral-examination/
- The ABNS primary written exam is now a mastery exam intended to demonstrate core knowledge. It is 375 multiple-choice questions, usually administered in March, and must be passed for credit before the chief year. As of July 1, 2025, residents must master the neuroanatomy exam before registering for the primary exam for credit or self-assessment. Source: https://abns.org/primary-examination/
- AANS Young Neurosurgeons News summarizes the 2017 oral-board change similarly: three 45-minute sessions, including general neurosurgery, chosen focus or general, and candidate cases; POST requires 125 consecutive operative cases, with selected cases reviewed during the exam. Source: https://newsletters.aans.org/ync/winter-2019/neurosurgery-oral-boards-an-early-experience-with-the-abns-post-case-entry-system/

## Preparation Principles

- Repeated mock oral examinations are widely used because they force learners to practice verbal clinical reasoning, poise, and defense of decisions under stress.
- In a radiation-oncology mock oral intervention, all participating residents reported increased clinical knowledge and comfort with clinical reasoning, and residents/faculty requested more mock oral experiences. The authors noted future improvement targets: broader disease sites, better emulation of the exam, consolidated formats, and grading. Source: https://bmcmededuc.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12909-020-02106-4
- The same paper notes that oral boards require clinical expertise plus confidence and poise to explain rationale in a high-stress setting. Source: https://bmcmededuc.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12909-020-02106-4
- A general-surgery mock oral study found residents perceived mock oral examinations as very or extremely helpful even when mock performance did not directly correlate with board passage. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26686905/
- Virtual mock oral literature supports feasibility, cost/time efficiency, and examiner heterogeneity for exam-day realism. Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7934051/
- A senior-surgery pilot curriculum used repeated board-style exams with faculty examiners and peer audience over months, supporting longitudinal rather than single-session preparation. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35472726/

## Design Implications For This Skill

- Simulate staged disclosure rather than dumping a full case.
- Weight safety, clinical judgment, and rationale more heavily than trivia.
- Require the learner to speak a plan, defend alternatives, and rescue complications.
- Include common dangerous diagnoses first; use zebras as discriminators or twist points, not as the whole purpose of the exercise.
- Provide rapid coaching for PGY-1 mode and delayed feedback for board-mode.
- Use memory to progress from first-principles recognition toward independent junior-staff reasoning over years.
