# Intraoperative Guide Attending Question Bank

Curated procedure-family pimping questions used by the expert completeness reviewer (`intraoperative-guide-review.md`) as an independent cross-check. The reviewer pulls relevant family-tagged questions in addition to generating its own fresh attending-defense questions.

The bank is intentionally **family-level**, not procedure-level: each question should apply broadly within its family. Conditional tags (`[fusion-only]`, `[aneurysm-only]`, `[awake-only]`, etc.) mark questions the reviewer should skip when not applicable rather than scrubbing depth from the bank.

The bank emphasizes attending-style readiness: OR-team choreography, Pre-Scrub Mental Rehearsal, multi-family procedure review, concrete crisis handling, and false-completeness detection.

---

## Universal Questions (apply to every neurosurgical procedure)

1. Walk me through your decision to operate. Why now, why this operation, and what is the natural history if we do nothing — at 6 months, 2 years, 10 years?
2. Defend your approach against the most reasonable alternative. What anatomy, pathology, host factor, or outcome data drove the choice? When would you flip to the alternative mid-decision?
3. What did you see on imaging that a less-experienced reader would miss, and what would change your plan?
4. Anesthesia asks: what targets — MAP, CPP, paCO2, paralytic posture, brain relaxation, neuromonitoring compatibility, transfusion threshold, glucose target — do you want for this case and why?
5. Consent: name the three risks you quote with numbers, the two you describe qualitatively, and what alternative you offer. What is your script if the patient declines surgery?
6. Walk me through OR setup from the time the patient rolls in: what is each team member doing, what is your time-out script, what is the verbal "stop" signal for any team member, and what triggers a hard reset?
7. Name the three highest-yield mistakes a PGY-2 will make on this case and the verbal cue you use to prevent each.
8. Name the three things that must be true before you close. How do you confirm each intraoperatively with a named tool, image, or test?
9. Anticipate the most likely intraoperative crisis. Walk me through your bail-out from immediate action through transfusion threshold, calling for help, and the verbal cue that triggers escalation.
10. Equipment fails mid-case — drill, microscope, navigation, neuromonitoring, suction. Pick one and walk me through your contingency without aborting.
11. Postop hour 4: the patient looks worse. What is your differential, what is your first action, and how does each finding map back to a specific intraoperative step?
12. Differentiate the two most commonly confused postoperative complications for this procedure by timing, exam signature, and imaging signature. What is the threshold for re-exploration vs medical management?
13. What evidence — RCT, registry, large series, guideline — supports this operation? Quote at least one effect size or comparator number that defends your choice.
14. What patient-specific modifier — host factor, anatomic variant, prior surgery, medication, frailty index — would change your conduct, and what changes? Pick the one most relevant to this case.
15. What goes in your op note that is specific to this procedure (not generic boilerplate)? What does the chief resident need to read at 2 a.m. to make a correct decision about a new postop change?
16. Why does each major step occur in the order it does? Pick two steps and trace mechanical or anatomic rationale forward (what this step enables) and backward (what would fail if you skipped it).
17. What would a chief resident notice in the first 60 seconds of the case that you might be missing? (Meta-question to expose false completeness.)

## Vascular (aneurysm, AVM, AVF, bypass, carotid endarterectomy/stenting, vertebral, EC-IC)

1. Why definitive treatment now and why this technique (clip / coil / flow-divert / endarterectomy / bypass / embolize) for this lesion? Cite the outcome data that defends it.
2. Define your proximal and distal control plan before you enter the cistern, retroperitoneum, or neck. What is the abort criterion if control is inadequate?
3. The lesion ruptures, dissects, or hemorrhages during exposure. Walk me from immediate proximal control through definitive treatment with verbal cues to anesthesia and the scrub.
4. Describe your hemostasis hierarchy for a perforator, branch vessel, and parent vessel — each separately.
5. Intraoperative flow/patency confirmation tool of choice (ICG / microdoppler / intraop angiography / intraop ultrasound). When in the sequence do you use it, and what finding triggers reposition or revision? [aneurysm: clip reposition; AVM: nidus residual; bypass: graft patency; carotid: residual stenosis]
6. Neuromonitoring signal drops 50% after a critical maneuver. Walk me through your algorithm in order: anesthetic check, BP, position, clip/clamp/flow, packing, retractor, temperature.
7. Defend temporary occlusion (clipping, clamping, balloon) vs adenosine vs rapid ventricular pacing vs systemic anticoagulation as your proximal control adjunct here. [aneurysm/bypass/carotid: pick one or combination]
8. Adjacent perforator or branch preservation — what is your "do not chase" rule, and what injury signature would you see postoperatively?
9. Postop new deficit at hour 6 — perforator stroke, embolic stroke, vasospasm, mass effect, hyperperfusion, or seizure? Differentiate by timing, exam pattern, and imaging signature. Threshold for re-exploration vs intervention vs medical?
10. What modifier — Hunt-Hess/WFNS grade, anatomic variant (fetal PCA, accessory branches, anomalous origin), age, dominance, anticoagulation, multiplicity, atherosclerotic disease — most changes conduct in this case? How?
11. Defend your antiplatelet/anticoagulation strategy peri-operatively. What is the threshold to hold, bridge, or resume?
12. What is your specimen handling plan if pathology is unexpected (e.g., infectious aneurysm, hemorrhagic AVM mimic)?

## Spine (decompression, fusion, deformity, trauma, spine tumor, intradural)

1. Defend anterior vs posterior vs combined for this pathology. What alignment, compression vector, instability, and bone biology data drove the decision?
2. Wrong-level surgery prevention — what is your protocol from preoperative imaging review through dural exposure, including the specific intraoperative imaging you obtain and when?
3. Define decompression endpoint. What confirms "good enough" vs "more bone, disc, ligament, or capsule must come out"? What is your intraoperative test for residual compression?
4. Dural tear under the microscope mid-decompression. Walk me from immediate recognition through primary repair, patch, sealant, fibrin glue, fat/fascia/muscle plug, lumbar drainage decision, and postoperative surveillance.
5. Hardware/implant choice — defend your construct length, screw size, cage profile, graft material, biologics. What changes for osteoporotic bone, prior radiation, revision, or pediatric anatomy? [fusion/instrumentation cases]
6. Vascular structure at risk for this approach (vertebral artery, segmental arteries, iliacs, great vessels, anterior radicular artery). Where is it, how do you control it, what is the rescue if injured?
7. Neuromonitoring change in MEP, SSEP, EMG, or D-wave — algorithm in order: anesthetic, position, blood pressure, hardware, instrumentation, retractor, dura, cord manipulation. When do you stop?
8. Postoperative new neurologic deficit at 6 hours. Differential, imaging threshold, and re-exploration trigger. Differentiate hematoma, hardware impingement, root retraction, kink, ischemia.
9. Adjacent segment disease, pseudarthrosis, junctional kyphosis — incidence, recognition, surveillance imaging, and how your construct choice affects each. [fusion/deformity]
10. Define your "do not enter" zone for this approach (e.g., transverse foramen for cervical, segmental territory for thoracic, iliac vessels for L4-L5 anterior). What is the warning sign that you are too close?
11. What contemporary evidence (e.g., SPORT, Lenke, CSORN, Spine Patient Outcomes, MISS vs open) supports your conduct here?
12. What goes in your op note that the next surgeon needs to know for revision (graft type, level confirmation method, dural integrity, screw lengths, neuromonitoring events)?

## Skull Base (endonasal, lateral, posterior fossa skull base, transcranial-transorbital, combined)

1. Defend your corridor (endonasal vs supraorbital vs pterional vs subtemporal vs presigmoid/petrosal vs far lateral vs transcondylar vs orbitozygomatic) for this lesion. What anatomy, pathology, vascular relationship, or outcome data drove it?
2. CSF leak strategy — intraoperative recognition, multi-layer reconstruction technique, vascularized vs free flap rationale, lumbar drain decision, postoperative surveillance, and recognition of delayed leak.
3. Cavernous sinus, jugular foramen, or venous lake bleeding — your packing/coagulation/hemostatic agent ladder, the verbal "stop and pack" cue, and when you accept residual tumor to control hemorrhage.
4. Cranial nerve at risk for this approach. Anatomy, function, monitoring modality, threshold for signal change, response algorithm, rescue, and expected postoperative deficit pattern.
5. Vidian nerve, internal maxillary artery, ICA segments, optic nerve, abducens within Dorello, jugular bulb, sigmoid sinus — name the corridors you respect and how navigation, Doppler, or landmark confirmation prevents catastrophic injury.
6. Define resection endpoint. What does intraoperative imaging (intraop MRI, navigation update, endoscopic visualization, angiography) buy you for this operation, and what triggers you to stop short?
7. Postoperative hyponatremia, diabetes insipidus, hypopituitarism, visual decline, or cranial nerve palsy — differentiate and act. What lab/imaging timing? [sellar/parasellar/optic-pathway]
8. Reconstruction flap — which (nasoseptal, pericranial, temporalis, free flap), what is your contingency if the planned flap is unavailable or insufficient, and what changes for a radiated bed or prior surgery?
9. Air embolism, venous infarction, sigmoid sinus injury, or tension pneumocephalus — recognition signatures and rescue ladder.
10. What modern outcome data (extent of resection thresholds, biochemical remission rates, cranial nerve preservation, recurrence, complication-free survival) defends your operation? Cite at least one comparator.
11. Anatomic-variant warning pattern on preoperative imaging that would change conduct (e.g., ICA crossing midline, anomalous carotid loop, dehiscent optic canal, dural sinus dominance).
12. Defend your decision to operate vs radiosurgery vs observation. What candidacy threshold, growth rate, or symptom pattern crosses the line?

## Tumor (cranial — glial, meningioma, metastasis, pituitary, pineal, intraventricular, eloquent-cortex)

1. Why operate vs observe vs radiosurgery vs systemic therapy vs biopsy-only? Cite the data that defends your choice for this lesion and patient.
2. Maximize safe resection — what tools (awake mapping, fMRI, DTI/tractography, 5-ALA, intraop MRI, intraop ultrasound, navigation, fluorescein, electrocorticography) do you use here, in what order, and what changes if any becomes unavailable mid-case?
3. Eloquent cortex or white-matter tract proximity — your mapping strategy, signal/behavioral threshold for "you are too close," and the explicit decision rule for stopping resection. [eloquent/awake]
4. Patient has a hypervascular tumor. Defend your hemostasis plan from preoperative embolization decision through devascularization sequence and the "do not chase" rule for residual feeders.
5. Define your resection endpoint for this histology and location. What is "gross total" vs "supratotal" vs "subtotal" in this case, and what mapping/imaging tool confirms it?
6. Postoperative swelling, hemorrhage into the resection bed, seizure, or new deficit at 6 hours — recognition, imaging threshold, anti-edema escalation (steroid → hypertonic → mannitol → re-operation), and re-exploration trigger.
7. CSF diversion need — when do you place an EVD intraoperatively, leave a ventriculostomy, or plan elective shunt? What is the postop trigger to convert to permanent diversion?
8. Specimen handling — frozen vs touch prep vs permanent vs molecular markers vs flow cytometry vs cultures. What do you send, in what order, and what affects intraop decisions before final pathology returns?
9. How does intraoperative histology change conduct (lymphoma → stop and biopsy; high-grade glioma → push for margin; metastasis → en bloc; abscess → drain and culture)?
10. Modifier: prior radiation, NF1/NF2, BRCA/mismatch repair, anticoagulation, age extremes, performance status, eloquent location. How does each change conduct?
11. What contemporary evidence (gross-total vs subtotal extent thresholds, awake vs asleep mapping, radiation timing, MGMT-driven decisions, immunotherapy adjuvants, RANO-BM criteria) defends your operation?
12. Family communication before molecular results — what do you commit to and what do you defer?

## Functional / Epilepsy / Movement Disorders / Pain / Neuromodulation

1. Defend operation over medical management. Cite candidacy criteria (e.g., AAN DBS criteria, ILAE drug-resistant epilepsy, FBSS criteria) and effect-size evidence.
2. Target localization strategy — imaging, electrophysiology, atlas, microelectrode recording, intraoperative stimulation testing. Which is non-negotiable for this target, and what changes if one modality fails?
3. Awake patient management — communication choreography, stop criteria, seizure rescue, panic/conversion rescue, conversion-to-general criteria. [awake-only]
4. Trajectory planning to avoid vessel, ventricle, eloquent transgression, and bone variance. How do you confirm trajectory before passing? What is your re-plan rule?
5. Intraop test stimulation — what response confirms target (motor capture, paresthesia, mood change, side effect threshold) and what response triggers repositioning?
6. Bleeding during electrode, lead, paddle, probe, or laser fiber placement — recognition (clinical change, imaging, MER quieting) and rescue.
7. Hardware-related complications — infection, migration, lead fracture, generator pocket erosion, IPG flip. Incidence, recognition, salvage vs explant decision.
8. Postoperative speech, motor, cognitive, or mood change — differentiate edema, microhemorrhage, target misplacement, stim-related, or expected transient effect.
9. Programming/staging logistics that affect operative decisions today (lead position margin, generator pocket choice, bilateral staging vs same-day, RC+ vs RC- compatibility for MRI).
10. What evidence supports this operation (RCT, registry, long-term effect-size durability, comparator data, quality-of-life metrics)?
11. Define operative completion — final stimulation thresholds, imaging confirmation of lead position, generator implantation criteria.
12. What modifier — cognitive impairment, depression, social support, expectation calibration, comorbid pain or movement disorder — alters your decision to offer surgery?

## Peripheral Nerve (entrapment release, nerve tumor, brachial/lumbosacral plexus, traumatic repair, nerve transfer)

1. Why operate now? Cite electrodiagnostic (NCS/EMG/SNAP/CMAP), ultrasound/MRI, and clinical thresholds, time-from-injury, and what the natural history would do without intervention.
2. Confirm the correct nerve and the correct level/segment before incision. What surface anatomy, imaging, ultrasound, and intraoperative confirmation steps?
3. Intraoperative stimulation strategy — proximal-to-distal mapping, threshold for "viable fascicle," decision rule for split repair vs cable graft vs nerve transfer.
4. Distinguish neurolysis vs resection vs end-to-end repair vs interposition graft vs nerve transfer here. Cite outcome data (e.g., MRC grade recovery, time-to-reinnervation) for each.
5. Hemostasis in the brachial plexus, popliteal fossa, carotid sheath, or thoracic outlet corridor — control points, microvascular preservation, what you do for major-vessel injury.
6. Postoperative new motor or sensory deficit — differentiate retraction neuropraxia, hematoma compression, ischemia, or technical injury. Imaging and EMG timing.
7. What modifier — diabetic neuropathy, autoimmune (vasculitis, CIDP), anticoagulation, prior surgery, radiation, smoking — changes operative decision-making and expected recovery?
8. Donor-nerve morbidity expectations if you transfer or graft (sural, MABC, AIN-PIN, lateral antebrachial cutaneous). Counsel and document.
9. Define your "operation complete" criterion intraoperatively. What named test (stimulation threshold, dimensions of decompression, tension-free coaptation, motor response) confirms adequacy?
10. Contemporary evidence — what defines a good outcome at 6, 12, 24 months for this operation? Cite a trial or registry.
11. Anatomic variant or warning pattern that would change conduct (anomalous innervation, Martin-Gruber, accessory branch, prior surgery scarring, congenital variant).
12. Specimen handling for nerve tumor (en bloc vs intracapsular, frozen, immunohistochemistry, neurofibromatosis screen). What do you send and what affects intraop choice?

## Pediatric (congenital, peds hydrocephalus, peds tumor, peds trauma, craniosynostosis, peds spine)

1. Anesthetic and physiologic considerations unique to this age — airway, IV access, blood loss tolerance (mL/kg), temperature regulation, glucose, ETT cuff, vasoactive readiness. What changes from adult conduct?
2. Surgical timing relative to developmental milestones, weight thresholds, comorbidities, and family readiness. What is the youngest you would offer this operation and why?
3. Cranial vault, suture, fontanelle, and dural maturation considerations that change exposure, hemostasis, and closure (e.g., dural adherence to suture in infants, vault thickness for screws).
4. Imaging strategy in a child — radiation/sedation tradeoffs, fast MRI protocols, intraop adjuncts, and decision thresholds (e.g., ETV success score, ICP monitoring threshold).
5. Hemostasis in a small circulating volume — transfusion thresholds (mL/kg), TXA dosing, antifibrinolytic strategy, scalp bleeding control, hypotensive anesthesia caution. What does "10% blood loss" actually mean here?
6. Hydrocephalus-specific: ETV success score, shunt valve choice (programmable, anti-siphon, antibiotic-impregnated), CSF sampling protocol, programmable-valve interaction with MRI. [hydrocephalus]
7. Postoperative seizure, fever, irritability, fontanelle change, or new neurologic deficit — differentiate normal recovery from operative complication, vasospasm, infection, hardware issue. Timing.
8. Modifier: syndromic patient (NF1/2, tuberous sclerosis, Apert, Crouzon, Down, Chiari, Klippel-Feil, Sturge-Weber). How does syndrome change conduct? Pick the most relevant for this case.
9. Family communication and consent specifics — capacity, surrogate decision-making, palliative trajectory, expected disability, future-operation likelihood.
10. What contemporary pediatric evidence (HCRN, COG, MAGIC, CNS surveillance protocols, craniosynostosis registries) supports your conduct?
11. Equipment scaling — pediatric-specific instruments, pediatric drills, infant retractors, pediatric monitoring leads. What do you confirm preoperatively?
12. Specimen handling and tumor-board pathway — what is sent, what triggers immediate oncology involvement, what is the consent for biobanking?

## CSF / Hydrocephalus / Trauma / Bedside / ICP Management (EVD, lumbar drain, shunt, hematoma evacuation, decompressive craniectomy, ICP monitoring)

1. Indication and timing — what numbers (ICP, GCS, midline shift, hematoma volume), imaging findings, or clinical trajectory triggered this operation, and what would have made you wait?
2. Coagulation/platelet/anticoagulant management before drilling, dural opening, or catheter insertion. What labs do you require, what is your reversal protocol, and what is the threshold to proceed urgently anyway?
3. Sterile technique discipline — verbal time-out script, do-not-touch zone, drape integrity, hand-hygiene cadence, and what triggers a hard reset.
4. Entry point and trajectory — surface landmarks, neuronavigation when used, intraoperative imaging, wrong-side prevention. What is the named technique to confirm correct level/side?
5. Catheter or burr-hole problems: bleeding through the catheter, no-CSF return, brain herniation through trephine, sudden ICP swing, contralateral pupil change. Recognition and immediate action.
6. Drainage system management — height referencing/transducer zero, sampling discipline, infection prevention bundle, CSF protein/glucose/cell trends that change management.
7. Postoperative deterioration after EVD, shunt, or evacuation — recognize over-drainage, slit-ventricle, malposition, contralateral or new hematoma, pneumocephalus, tension pneumocephalus, seizure. Timing.
8. Decompressive craniectomy — bone-flap size logic (minimum diameter), dural opening pattern (stellate vs cruciate), expansile duroplasty, cranioplasty timing/material, and contemporary outcome evidence (DECRA, RESCUEicp). [decompressive only]
9. When does an EVD convert to a permanent shunt? What predicts shunt dependence (etiology, infection history, CSF protein, weaning trial response)?
10. What modifier (age, frailty, malignancy, advance directives, family goals of care) changes whether you offer this operation, and how do you counsel and document?
11. Drug thresholds at the bedside — mannitol vs hypertonic saline (concentration and dose), sedation choice, antiepileptic prophylaxis, antibiotic timing. Pick the most relevant.
12. Specimen and culture handling for suspected infection or unexpected tumor on bedside drainage. What is sent, when, and what affects the next operative decision?

## Reviewer Use

The reviewer should:

1. **Identify the primary procedure family.** Use the procedure title and decomposition to pick one. If the procedure spans two families (e.g., far lateral approach = skull base + posterior-fossa-tumor; bypass = vascular + sometimes tumor or skull base), pick both as primary + secondary.

2. **Pull questions:**
   - All **17 universal questions** apply.
   - Pull **at least 6 of the 12 primary-family questions**. Pull more when the procedure is broad within its family.
   - Pull **at least 3 of the 12 secondary-family questions** if a secondary family applies.
   - Skip conditional-tag questions when the tag does not apply (e.g., skip `[fusion-only]` for a decompression-only spine procedure). Record the skip rationale in the verdict JSON.

3. **Confirm each pulled question is addressed** in the draft. A question is *addressed* when the guide either:
   - Contains explicit content that would let a resident answer attending-style under pressure, including mechanism or threshold, not just terminology; OR
   - Explicitly states why the question does not apply to this procedure with reasoning.
   
   Heading-text matches are not sufficient. Surface-level mention without mechanism, threshold, or named tool counts as "not addressed."

4. **Record in the verdict JSON.** Add pulled-and-addressed questions to `fresh_attending_questions` in `expert-review-cycle-<N>.json`. Add unaddressed questions to `blocking_gaps` with `rubric_block` set to the question topic.

5. **Generate additional questions independently.** The bank is reference material, not an exhaustive list. Generate at least 3 of your own questions specific to the procedure under review, as required by the review module.

The bank exists to prevent the writer from gaming the review by pre-tuning the draft to the questions the writer itself generated during decomposition. The reviewer must always also produce its own fresh questions, but the bank floors the cross-check.
